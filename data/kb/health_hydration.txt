Hydration means maintaining enough body water for normal function. Daily fluid needs vary by body size,
weather, diet, activity level, and health conditions.

Practical hydration tips:
- Drink water regularly through the day instead of waiting until very thirsty.
- Keep a water bottle nearby at work, travel, and during study sessions.
- Drink extra fluids during hot weather, exercise, fever, vomiting, or diarrhea.
- Include water-rich foods like fruits, vegetables, soups, and yogurt.
- Limit frequent high-sugar drinks; they can add calories without improving hydration habits.

Common signs of mild dehydration include dry mouth, darker yellow urine, headache, fatigue, dizziness,
and reduced urine output. Very dark urine, confusion, fast heartbeat, or inability to keep fluids down
can be warning signs that need medical attention.

A simple self-check: urine that is pale yellow is often a sign of better hydration, while consistently
dark yellow urine can indicate low fluid intake.
